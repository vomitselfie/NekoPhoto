# Background removal review: what the last six months offer

Five parallel reviews of background removal, September 2026, aimed at work
from Chinese groups published or released between March and September 2026:
models and their ONNX exports, the matting maths, mask refinement and
full-resolution tricks, click-based subject selection, and datasets and
metrics. Every item names its origin, date and licence, where it would land in
our code, the expected gain, and the effort in days. It is written like
`docs/pixel-math-review.md`, to be worked through batch by batch; the Progress
table fills as batches land.

How it was gathered, and what it could not reach: the session's web-search
budget was already spent, so everything comes from direct fetches of primary
sources (the arXiv, OpenAlex and Crossref APIs, GitHub, Hugging Face,
ModelScope, PaddleSeg and OpenCV source, the papers themselves, three of them
extracted locally). Baidu, CSDN, 知乎, 掘金 and the vendor blogs (稿定, 美图,
鹿班, 佐糖) were unreachable from this machine, and IEEE, Elsevier and MDPI full
texts returned 403, so the engineering write-ups that made the pixel-maths
sweep so productive are missing here and a few journal items are known by
title and abstract only (marked "unread"). Every link below was fetched and
checked. CPU costs are estimates from FLOPs and third-party datapoints unless
a measurement is cited, so treat them as ±2×.

The headline is that very little new Chinese work in the window is both usable
and CPU-feasible. The 2026 matting papers from Chinese groups went to SAM2/SAM3
prompting, diffusion and Mamba backbones, nearly all under non-commercial
licences (the "Not usable" list at the end); the 41 newest arXiv matting
entries and the 50 newest Crossref ones are all deep, and the one classical
item in the window is a side-window filter variant from Harbin whose full text
could not be read. What is worth taking is (a) selection heuristics from
2025–2026 papers, plain C++ against the model we already ship, promising the
largest visible gain, (b) older Chinese work on sampling and guided filters
that upgrades each stage of our matte, (c) a few exports and permissively
licensed models from 2022–2026, and (d) an evaluation set under MIT so that
every gain here becomes a number. Where the best available route is not
Chinese (Meta's EfficientSAM through OpenCV's own zoo, pymatting's foreground
estimation), it is listed and marked as such.

## What we have today

`src/core/src/subject.cpp` runs one ONNX file through OpenCV's DNN module:
IS-Net at 1024², the U2Net family at 320², PP-HumanSeg at 192², and any file
named `modnet*` at 512², each resized to a square with area sampling, the
saliency nets' output min-max stretched, the result resized back to the layer
with bilinear sampling. `refineMatte` in `matte.cpp` then applies the panel in
order: the guided filter (an R, G, B guide, edge-aware epsilon, coefficients on
a grid 2× or 4× coarser as in the fast guided filter, evaluated against the
full guide), the matting band (Gastal & Oliveira shared sampling: rays into
the sure regions, the best-explaining pair per pixel, pairs shared between
neighbours, a confidence- and colour-weighted smoothing), Shift Edge as
grey-level dilation or erosion, and Contrast. The dialog runs the model once
at full layer size on a worker thread, shows PP-HumanSeg's mask within
milliseconds when it is downloaded, refines at 1400 px for the preview and at
full size on commit. `ModelStore` lists four one-file models. Nothing estimates
the foreground colour, so half-transparent hair keeps the old background's
tint when composited over a new one.

The OpenCV that runs the model differs by build, and it decides what can ship:
the desktop here links 5.0.0, CI links Ubuntu 24.04's 4.6.0, the AppImage is
built on 22.04 and carries 4.5.4, the Mac bundle takes whatever Homebrew
ships. So a model has to import in 4.5's ONNX parser to reach AppImage users;
anything that needs a newer engine is opt-in and must hide itself when the
runtime cannot load it. The verified state of the parser: SAM ViT-B loads from
4.8, EfficientSAM from 4.10, SAM 2.1 from 5.0's new engine; 4.x rejects
`GatherND`, `GridSample`, most `Resize` modes and the quantised ops; 5.0 lifts
those but rejects `DeformConv` unless built with its new ONNX Runtime engine
(`ENGINE_ORT`), which would add onnxruntime to the runtime, a policy decision
this document does not recommend. (Since this review: CI, the release jobs and
the Mac bundle build OpenCV 4.14 themselves, `tools/build-opencv.sh`, so the
shipped runtime is one known version and the 4.8-and-later imports are open to
AppImage users.)

## Not usable, and why

- **SAM2Matting** (Fudan + SUFE, ECCV 2026, arXiv 2606.27339): CC BY-NC-SA 4.0, and Hiera/SAM3 backbones.
- **MatAnyone 2** (NTU S-Lab, CVPR 2026 highlight): S-Lab licence, non-commercial; video only, needs a first-frame mask.
- **LFNet** (Changzhou U., arXiv 2606.26849) and **U²Mamba** (arXiv 2606.20282): Mamba selective scan has no ONNX or OpenCV path.
- **RenderMatte** (arXiv 2608.08487), **ReChannel** (arXiv 2607.06553), **OrienDiffusion** (Beijing U. of Technology, IJCAI 2026), **CineMatte** (arXiv 2605.18328), **Parallax Portrait Matting** (CUHK, arXiv 2607.11205, needs two frames): FLUX or diffusion scale, or extra captures. The same goes for every 2026 hair-specific paper (HairGuard, αDepth): all deep, nothing classical to lift.
- **SSMatte** (HUST + Beijing Normal U., arXiv 2607.10395, Jul 2026): self-supervised DINOv2-S + ViTMatte-S, P3M SAD 17.17 with no manual labels, but code "will be available", licence unknown, 534 GFLOPs at 1024². Watch.
- **RBIM** (Harbin Institute of Technology, Apr 2026): an Apache-2.0 placeholder repo claiming AIM-500 SAD 12.0; no paper, weights or architecture. Watch.
- **HVPNet-lite** (Jiangxi Normal U., arXiv 2606.31496, Jun 2026): 3.6 M params, 3.4 GFLOPs at 352², plain convolutions, so it would import anywhere, but the repo has no licence file and only saliency numbers at 352². Ask the authors; watch.
- **Edge-tangent-flow side-window guided filter** (Liu & Cui, Harbin U. of Science and Technology, Applied Sciences, 14 Jul 2026, CC BY 4.0, DOI 10.3390/app16147058): the only classical in-window item; +5.7 dB on denoising benchmarks, nothing on matting, and the full text was unreachable. Tangent-aligned filtering is the natural way to keep strands coherent, so revisit once readable.
- **Adaptive dynamic guided filter** (Long et al., Signal Processing, Feb 2026, DOI 10.1016/j.sigpro.2025.110290): in the window, Chinese, unread; per-pixel adaptive regularisation, which our edge-aware epsilon already is, so a low expected delta. Two Chinese-journal papers that, like us, start from a coarse semantic mask and solve foreground, background and alpha together (Wang & Zhang, 计算机辅助设计与图形学学报 2022, DOI 10.3724/SP.J.1089.2022.19175; 姚桂林 et al., 中国科学: 信息科学 2021, DOI 10.1360/ssi-2020-0121) could not be fetched; leads for a day with journal access.
- **Evolutionary sample-pair search** (Liang, Huang, Cai et al., SCUT + UESTC Zhongshan, 2019–2025, the last in Swarm & Evolutionary Computation, Oct 2025): the only sustained Chinese classical-matting line, solving item 8's search by particle swarms; slower than the PatchMatch search and hard to make deterministic. Contents unread.
- **MEMatte** (BJTU + Meitu MT Lab, AAAI 2025, MIT): the one verified Meitu-linked pipeline, but trimap-based, and its data-dependent token routing does not export cleanly.
- **SAMRefiner** (ZJU + Shanghai AI Lab + HKU, ICLR 2025, MIT): training-free mask-to-prompt refinement, but needs SAM ViT-H and returns its low-resolution mask.
- **LawDIS, PDFNet, MVANet** as models: an SD2 U-Net (3.5 B), Swin-B + Depth Anything (about 60 s per image on CPU), Swin-B with four local views. Their heuristics are item 7.
- **Mask2Alpha** (arXiv 2502.17093): no code, no affiliation. **Upsample Anything** (KAIST/Microsoft): per-image optimised joint bilateral upsampling with no evidence of beating a guided filter. **RaysUp** (Tongji): for ViT features, needs training. **Steering-kernel weighted guided filters** (Sun et al., TIP 2020; Jia et al. 2024): a per-pixel eigen-decomposition, 5–10× the cost, no matting gain shown.
- **EdgeSAM** (S-Lab + Shanghai AI Lab): the best small SAM, non-commercial. **MobileSAM2** (Xidian + PolyU + NTU, Jul 2026) and **TinySAM 2** (Huawei, May 2026): no code or weights. **FastSAM** (CASIA): AGPL-3.0, not point-promptable. **Lite-SAM**, **SAM-Lightening**, **SAMA**: no usable code.
- **Official MODNet weights**: CC BY-NC-SA 4.0 (PaddleSeg's are Apache-2.0, item 11). **RVM** (ByteDance): GPL-3.0; its downsample-then-deep-guided-filter recipe is what our fast guided filter already does. **RMBG-2.0** (BRIA): non-commercial. **BiRefNet full, HR and dynamic**: 215 M params, and the native-`DeformConv` exports need the ONNX Runtime engine.
- **FCLM** (HKUST-GZ, AAAI 2026): code withheld by the company. **PSUNet** (OPPO, 4.15 M params): no weights.
- **OpenCV `alphamat`** (information-flow matting, Apache-2.0): a dense global affinity solve with no published runtimes, and the module is not linked. **np-csu/AlphaMatting** (C++ shared matting) and **YuanhaoGong/SideWindowFilter**: no licence, so reference only.
- **DIS5K**, **PPM-100**, **Transparent-460**, **ThinObject-5K**: non-commercial data, for local probes only, never in the repo. **HIM2K**: no licence stated. **Composition-1k**, **Distinctions-646**: email-only, not redistributable. **MaSS13K** (arXiv 2503.18364, 4K masks) says CC BY 4.0 on the abstract page; worth one follow-up as the only permissive thin-structure set.
- Already ours, re-derived by two of the reviews before they read `matte.cpp`: the fast guided filter (subsampled coefficients, He & Sun 2015) and the deep-guided-filter lift RVM uses; grey-level Shift Edge; the shared-sampling band. laviewpbt's only matting post (2014) documents that band as we have it, including its known failure, samples lost when a known region is narrower than the ray step, which item 8 fixes.

## Suggested order

1. The harness (item 10, 2 d) so that everything after it is measured, and the gaps table (1 d).
2. The model-independent matte upgrades (items 1–4, about 4 d): foreground estimation, the connectivity cleanup, the gradient-domain term, the band rule. Visible in every composite.
3. Global sampling in the band (item 8, 2–4 d): the direct fix for the hair the rays miss.
4. The boundary-window experiment, then the feature if the ten images say yes (item 7, 1 + 3–5 d): the largest hair and thin-structure gain available with the model we ship.
5. Scribble selection (item 9, 2–4 d): the offline fallback and the fix for the model's failures.
6. The model entries, INT8 IS-Net and the PaddleSeg portrait nets (items 5 and 11, 2½ d); flip averaging, the band-only side windows, band narrowing and the high-pass mode (items 6, 13, 14, 15) only where the harness asks for them.
7. The OpenCV decision: build the three modules from source in the release image, which unblocks everything from 4.8 on for AppImage users; then click-to-select (item 16 with 17 to prove it, 18 or 19 as the Chinese model).
8. BiRefNet lite behind the OpenCV 5 check (item 12), the nonlocal and closed-form band solves (20–21) and the learned refiners (22–24) only if the harness shows items 7 and 8 leaving something on the table.
